What Is a Sequence?

A sequence is an ordered list of numbers, often generated by a rule or pattern. Each number in the sequence is called a term, and the position is given by an index, usually n.

Sequences can be defined in two common ways:

  • Explicitly: A formula gives the value of any term directly
  • Recursively: Each term depends on previous ones
Sequences have been studied for thousands of years, with some of the earliest examples appearing in ancient Greek and Indian mathematics. One of the most famous sequences, the Fibonacci sequence, was introduced to Europe in the 1200s, although it had been known in India long before. As mathematics evolved, sequences became essential for understanding patterns, limits, and infinite processes, playing a key role in the development of calculus. Today, sequences show up in many areas — from computer algorithms and financial models to physics, biology, and engineering. Studying sequences helps us recognize patterns, make predictions, and solve complex problems, making them a fundamental tool in both pure and applied mathematics.

Why Sequences Matter

Sequences are the building blocks of many mathematical ideas—from calculus and algorithms to physics and biology.

Learning to work with sequences helps you:

  • Identify patterns
  • Write general formulas
  • Determine long-term behavior
  • Model real-world systems

Key Concepts and Theorems

Convergence vs. Divergence

A sequence converges if its terms get closer and closer to a fixed number L as n approaches infinity.
